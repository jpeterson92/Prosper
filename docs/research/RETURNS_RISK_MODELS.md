## Scope and conclusion

Prosper needs covariance/volatility and benchmark-relative risk estimates for an ordinary U.S. stock sleeve, with selected cash treated consistently. It does not need an expected-return forecast to estimate risk. Returns plus qualified industry classifications support serious estimators; missing commercial style exposures do not force use of an unregularized sample matrix. Vendor-equivalence and accuracy on Prosper's actual universe are not established by this review.

My research recommendation is to compare dynamic nonlinear shrinkage with a parsimonious market/latent-factor model whose residual covariance is also regularized, while retaining linear shrinkage as a reference and an industry-informed model as an interpretable challenger. This is a proposed experiment, not a selected production stack or a finding that every variant outperforms. D030's accepted shrinkage direction, open D012/D017 and G1-G3 remain unchanged.

## Focused reading list

The following are primary papers, author manuscripts or author/institutional publication records. Publication years are distinguished from working-paper versions. Some journal/author PDF retrievals were unavailable; those entries use accessible abstract/indexed paper excerpts and corroborating primary publication records. Full sections were accessible for the 2025 TE study, 2022 OHLC study and arXiv HTML studies. This is a focused literature review, not an exhaustive systematic review or independent replication.

| Ref | Paper and access | Method, evidence and relevance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| R01 | Ledoit & Wolf (2003), [Improved estimation of the covariance matrix of stock returns with an application to portfolio selection](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0927539803000070), Journal of Empirical Finance; (2004), [Honey, I Shrunk the Sample Covariance Matrix](https://www.ledoit.net/honey_abstract.htm), Journal of Portfolio Management | Shrink sample covariance toward a single-index or constant-correlation target. The latter includes benchmark-relative empirical evidence. These targets differ from sklearn's scaled-identity LedoitWolf; a shared author label does not make the estimators identical. |
| R02 | Ledoit & Wolf (2017), [Nonlinear Shrinkage of the Covariance Matrix for Portfolio Selection: Markowitz Meets Goldilocks](https://ledoit.net/Goldilocks_RFS_2017.pdf), Review of Financial Studies | Shrink individual eigenvalues rather than use one global linear rule. Portfolio-selection-specific theory and historical equity tests support the method when dimension is comparable to observations; asymptotic optimality is conditional on assumptions. |
| R03 | Ledoit & Wolf (2020), [Analytical nonlinear shrinkage](https://www.ledoit.net/Analytical_AoS_2020.pdf), Annals of Statistics; (2022), [Quadratic shrinkage for large covariance matrices](https://doi.org/10.3150/20-BEJ1315), Bernoulli | Practical, scalable analytical alternatives to older numerical shrinkage. QIS treats inverse eigenvalues and accommodates dimension exceeding sample size. Their statistical-loss/Monte-Carlo results are not direct proof of superior TE forecasts on Prosper's constrained portfolios. |
| R04 | Engle, Ledoit & Wolf (2019), [Large Dynamic Covariance Matrices](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/07350015.2017.1345683), Journal of Business & Economic Statistics | DCC-NL combines conditional volatility/correlation dynamics with nonlinear shrinkage and handles large stock universes. Returns-only data suffice for this model family; implementation and horizon validation are more demanding than static shrinkage. |
| R05 | De Nard, Ledoit & Wolf (2021), [Factor Models for Portfolio Selection in Large Dimensions: The Good, the Better and the Ugly](https://www.uni.li/en/publications/2021/factor-models-portfolio-selection-large-dimensions-good-better-and-ugly), Journal of Financial Econometrics; [author manuscript record](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4368040) | Combines factor structure with dynamic, regularized residual covariance. The important alternative to a strict factor model is retaining residual dependence rather than automatically making all residual covariances zero. |
| R06 | De Nard & Zhao (2023), [Using, taming or avoiding the factor zoo? A double-shrinkage estimator for covariance matrices](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0927539823000130), Journal of Empirical Finance 72, 23-35; [author description](https://denard.ch/Research/Publications/) | Regularizes factor coefficients and covariance components. Reported tests find a market factor or leading latent factors sufficient for their estimator; large observed factor sets are unnecessary in those experiments. This directly addresses absent style descriptors, without proving latent factors always dominate. |
| R07 | Fan, Liao & Mincheva (2013), [Large Covariance Estimation by Thresholding Principal Orthogonal Complements](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3859166/), JRSS B | POET combines principal-component common factors with a thresholded, conditionally sparse residual covariance. Provides theory and portfolio-allocation evidence. Factor-number/threshold choices and residual sparsity are assumptions to check, not guarantees supplied by daily returns. |
| R08 | Kakushadze (2015), [Heterotic Risk Models](https://arxiv.org/html/1508.04883v4), Wilmott | Local correlation PCs within a granular industry hierarchy plus nested factor covariance. Strong input compatibility; the reported intraday long/short tests use Yahoo prices with BICS and acknowledge survivorship bias. They are not validated long-only TE forecasts or a test of Yahoo's own taxonomy. |
| R09 | Tan & Zohren, [Estimation of Large Financial Covariances: A Cross-Validation Approach](https://arxiv.org/html/2012.05757v2), January 2023 arXiv version | EWA-CV combines exponential weighting and cross-validated nonlinear eigenvalue shrinkage. Large-universe results rival dynamic alternatives with less computational overhead. Their no-short-sales tests substantially narrow estimator differences. Treat the retrieved version as a working paper, not a verified journal publication. |
| R10 | De Nard, Engle, Ledoit & Wolf (2022), [Large dynamic covariance matrices: Enhancements based on intraday data](https://www.econ.uzh.ch/dam/jcr%3A5ee13ed0-14df-4562-9beb-086a2d76738f/jbf_2022.pdf), Journal of Banking & Finance | Uses daily OHLC summaries for better volatility proxies and regularized returns within DCC-NL. Does not require a full historical intraday tape. Attractive later extension, conditional on OHLC/action consistency; some empirical missing-data/forward-availability conventions conflict with Prosper's acceptance rules. |
| R11 | De Nard, Ledoit & Wolf (2025), [Improved Tracking-Error Management for Active and Passive Investing](https://www.econ.uzh.ch/dam/jcr%3A8f7e20cf-e9a7-4fde-87cb-aef82de26404/jpm_2025.pdf), Journal of Portfolio Management 51(4) | Highest direct task relevance: known/unknown benchmark weights, restricted universes and active tilts; evaluates TE calibration as well as outcomes, including long-only cases. Results favor nonlinear/dynamic shrinkage. Empirical conventions include zero filling and forward availability; do not import those into Prosper. |
| R12 | Jagannathan & Ma (2003), [Risk Reduction in Large Portfolios: Why Imposing the Wrong Constraints Helps](https://www.nber.org/papers/w8922), Journal of Finance | Constraints can regularize optimized portfolios and reduce the apparent advantage of sophisticated covariance estimators. Consequently evaluate the real constrained investor problem, not just unconstrained minimum-variance weights. |
| R13 | Patton (2011), [Volatility forecast comparison using imperfect volatility proxies](https://public.econ.duke.edu/~ap172/Patton_vol_proxies_JoE_2011.pdf), Journal of Econometrics | Noisy realized-risk proxies can change model rankings depending on the loss function. Supports robust forecast losses such as QLIKE with the required proxy assumptions, rather than ranking methods on a single realized volatility observation. |
| R14 | Fan, Liao & Shi (2015), [Risks of Large Portfolios](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4504849/), Journal of Econometrics | Portfolio-risk estimation uncertainty and H-CLUB bounds under factor-model assumptions. A useful later uncertainty-assessment reference; asymptotic confidence results do not certify arbitrary optimizer-selected trade baskets. |
| R15 | Bai & Ng (2002), [Determining the Number of Factors in Approximate Factor Models](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/1468-0262.00273), Econometrica | Data-driven factor-count criteria provide an alternative to choosing PCs solely by in-sample explained variance. Forecast-horizon and out-of-sample checks still matter. |
| R16 | Norges Bank Investment Management (2019), [Country and Industry Effects in Global Equity Returns](https://www.nbim.no/contentassets/70b80f2f81fb4a3dbd703dde9d568b0f/country-and-industry-effects-in-global-equity-returns.pdf), institutional discussion note | Uses the Heston-Rouwenhorst country/industry framework and shows varying importance over time. Useful primary institutional context, not a peer-reviewed universal covariance-estimator ranking. |

## Implications and proposed v2 experiment

Use the complete supported held/benchmark/candidate union to define required risk inputs; its size can greatly exceed the 20-200 held names and the fictional 500-name fixture. A broad reference universe can help estimate industry/statistical factors, but its membership, coverage and historic classifications require explicit qualification. Current classifications cannot be silently treated as point-in-time historical data.

Compare these bounded candidates on identical versioned data and horizons:

- Linear shrinkage and a market/industry factor reference with regularized factor covariance and specific variances.
- Static nonlinear shrinkage, including an appropriate analytical/QIS implementation.
- A conditional returns-only candidate: DCC-NL, or EWA-CV as a separately specified alternative. Simple volatility scaling of a shrunk correlation matrix is a useful baseline but is not identical to either published algorithm.
- A parsimonious market/latent-factor candidate with shrunk residual dependence, or an industry-heterotic challenger if taxonomy quality and group sizes support it. Merely adding PCs to industry dummies is not an implementation of the named heterotic paper.

For the current single-country domestic-equity scope, a constant U.S. dummy adds no independent cross-sectional country information beyond a market/intercept specification. Country/currency factors and asynchronous closes matter for a later international model; issuer domicile alone would not capture all geographic revenue/currency exposure. Return-derived beta/volatility/momentum or public factor-return regressions are possible later comparisons, not a prerequisite to covariance estimation or adopted style definitions.

My proposed selection criteria are rolling forward TE calibration, robust projected-variance forecast loss, performance on same-industry swaps and concentrated active baskets, stress underprediction, constrained-optimizer outcomes, model/weight stability and operational coverage. Predefine training/validation/test dates, factor counts, lookbacks/decays and horizons; select settings only within training and retain untouched outer test periods. Temporal/block-aware validation and uncertainty estimates are required; estimator-internal cross-fitting must not leak into the outer forward test. Assess both fixed portfolio weights and portfolios chosen using each model, since optimization can exploit estimation errors. Annualized daily TE is a scaling convention, not automatically a one-year conditional risk forecast.

Historical Yahoo/current-survivor data can support research but do not establish a survivorship-free backtest. Preserve action revisions/adjustment conventions and the [D040 cache/frozen-history contract](../domain/BENCHMARK_INPUTS.md#standard-benchmark-selection). Do not copy academic zero-fill, security deletion or future-availability shortcuts into the supported financial pipeline. Daily data also cannot by themselves qualify intraday execution risk or tail-loss guarantees.
